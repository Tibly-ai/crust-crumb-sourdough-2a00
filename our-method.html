The Slow Bread Manifesto

Good bread takes exactly 36 hours.

We do not use commercial yeast, dough conditioners, or speed up enzymes. We rely entirely on three simple variables: stone-ground flour, filtered water, and the natural passage of time.

01

Stone-Ground Milling

Our flour is sourced from family farms across the Pacific Northwest who prioritize soil health and heritage grain restoration. By choosing stone-milled grains, we retain the nutritious wheat germ and essential oils that modern roller mills strip away. This gives our loaves their characteristic dark, nutty aroma and golden crumb.

02

Feeding the Wild Yeast

The heart of our kitchen is a wild sourdough culture cultivated years ago. Every single morning, our bakers feed the starter with fresh flour and warm water, encouraging a vibrant community of wild yeasts and lactic acid bacteria to thrive. This culture is what coaxes the complex, slightly tangy flavors from the grain.

03

The 24-Hour Cold Sleep

After shaping the wet dough by hand, we place the loaves into natural cane banneton baskets and tuck them away in our cool retardation room for 24 hours. Under these cold conditions, the yeast activity slows to a crawl, while the lactic acid bacteria continue to break down heavy starches and gluten. This slow sleep is the secret behind a bread that is highly digestible and incredibly flavorful.

04

Hearth & Steam

At three in the morning, our bakers fire up the heavy stone-deck hearth ovens. Each loaf is tipped out of its proofing basket, hand-scored with a razor blade to guide its rise, and loaded directly onto the blistering stone. High-pressure steam is injected into the chamber to keep the outer skin soft, allowing the loaf to expand fully before the classic, deep-brown caramelized crust sets.

Experience It Fresh

Taste the difference patience makes.

Our wild sourdough loaves are baked fresh Wednesday through Sunday. Because we ferment slowly in small batches, quantities are limited. We pull our final loaves from the stone ovens right as our doors open at seven in the morning.
